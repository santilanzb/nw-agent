## 1. Position

The synthesis crowned C2 by **0.07 points** — a margin inside judge noise — on the strength of one lens (Precision/Reliability, 8.33 vs 7.67) whose differential rests on an integration behavior **that no public documentation describes** and that C2 itself lists as UNVERIFIED (candidate-C2.md §12.1–.2). Meanwhile C1 wins the largest single gap in the entire study (Pragmatism/Risk **8.0 vs 6.0**), wins Cost/Latency, and is — this matters — **the architecture the study's own orchestration researcher independently recommended.** R5's closing recommendation reads, verbatim: *"Keep the hand-rolled TurnFSM as the reactive router (it is an asset, not debt). Add DBOS Transact (Python) inside agent-core for: proactive cadences, multi-day sagas, and CRM-operator workflows"* (research-R5.md §3). That is not a paraphrase of C1; it **is** C1.

The two candidates are ~80% the same design (synthesis §1.2 concedes this). The tournament question is therefore narrow: for a 1–3 person team with a hard precision mandate, is it better to (a) keep a deployed, understood 300-line reactive router and apply durable execution *where the evidence says durability is needed* (time, sagas, writes), or (b) port the only validated behaviors onto a framework pairing whose hinge behavior is undocumented, whose agent framework shipped two majors in ~9 months and now promises breaking majors as fast as **every 3 months**, and whose durable substrate is a 2023-founded, seed-stage single-vendor whose versioning semantics — verified below — actively punish C2's own cadence design on every deploy?

New verified evidence gathered for this brief moves three of C2's "honest caveats" from *plausible risk* to *confirmed structural cost*. On the corrected record, C1 should take rank 1, with C2's four genuinely superior ideas grafted in (§4).

## 2. The best case for C1 (affirmative)

1. **It is the evidence-backed 2026 pattern at this scope, not a compromise.** The production consensus for customer-facing conversational agents is a thin owned loop (12-factor agents, https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents; Anthropic, https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents). R5's stress test concluded the FSM cracks on **time and exactly-once, not routing** — durable-execution territory, not agent-framework territory — and prescribed exactly C1's split: owned reactive loop + DBOS for proactive/saga/write workloads (research-R5.md §2–3). C2 must argue the consensus "flips at operator scope"; C1 just follows what the study's own research found.

2. **It wins the lenses that bind THIS business.** A 1–3 person ops team with no platform hire is the brief's stated constraint (§3). Pragmatism/Risk: C1 8.0, C2 6.0 — the widest gap in the scoreboard, and it points the other way from the verdict. The operator-pragmatist judge — the judge whose lens is closest to the buyer — ranked C1 first outright (7.6 vs 7.4). The overall margin for C2 (7.67 vs 7.60) is smaller than the disagreement between any two judges on almost any cell.

3. **Zero port of validated behavior.** F1/F3 are the only functions with production validation. C1 hardens them in place with the eval harness as a regression net; C2 must re-implement them in workflow form and gates the port on "eval parity + traffic replay" — its own High risk #2. The brief's precision mandate says sloppy-but-fast loses; a port of working conversational behavior is the single most gratuitous precision risk in either candidate, and C1 simply does not take it.

4. **Deploy-safe by design where C2 is deploy-fragile (new, verified).** C1's cadence engine keeps enrollment state in **relational rows** (`cadence_enrollments.next_run_at`, state machine) driven by `@DBOS.scheduled` ticks (candidate-C1.md L6). C2's cadence engine holds each lead's state inside a **week-long sleeping DBOS workflow** (candidate-C2.md L9). DBOS pins workflow recovery to an application version computed as a checksum of the code, and *"does not attempt to recover workflows tagged with a different application version"* — the documented remedies are blue-green deploys that keep **old-version worker processes running until all their workflows complete**, or manual fork/cancel (https://docs.dbos.dev/production/self-hosting/workflow-recovery). For workflows that sleep 3–7 days between touches, on a single 8GB droplet, during the exact 13-week window when the code churns weekly, that is a standing operational tax C2 never priced. C1's tick-over-tables design deploys freely: fresh code reads the same rows. (C1 uses durable sleep only for short sagas — quote follow-ups — where the drain window is days, not the lifetime of a cadence book.)

5. **Bounded framework exposure, same escape hatches.** C1 adopts DBOS narrowly: if DBOS-the-company disappears, C1 degrades to its priced SKIP-LOCKED fallback (~2 wks) and the reactive spine never notices. In C2, *everything* — every turn, touch, saga, write plan — is a DBOS workflow; a substrate failure is an existential base-layer event. Both candidates document the Temporal escape; only C1 can also simply *shrink* its dependency.

6. **Same cost, same rollout speed, same grafts.** ~10–12 vs ~11–14 engineer-weeks; identical cost model; every mandatory graft (G1 Chatwoot, G2 prices pull, G3 cadence content, G7 hygiene, G8 instrumentation, G9 single clock, G10 drift audit) slots onto C1 unchanged — the synthesis's own layer table shows the grafts attach to shared layers, not to C2-specific ones.

## 3. Attacks on C2 (each verified where marked)

### A1 — SERIOUS · The verdict's hinge is undocumented anywhere public, and C2 admits it
The synthesis: *"C2's margin exists if and only if the Stage-0 DBOSAgent spike holds"* (§1.1), and lists as stress-test item #1 the DBOSAgent deferred-tool pause/resume across restarts and deploy-versioning of in-flight workflows — *"both UNVERIFIED by C2's own admission."* Verified 2026-07-10: the official Pydantic AI DBOS integration page documents streaming, pickle-serialization, toolset-registration and retry caveats but **says nothing about deferred tools (`requires_approval`) inside a DBOS workflow** (https://pydantic.dev/docs/ai/integrations/durable_execution/dbos/); the DBOS-side integration page mentions "human-in-the-loop workflows" once in marketing copy and provides **no pause/resume mechanics, no versioning discussion** (https://docs.dbos.dev/integrations/pydantic-ai). C2's entire autonomy ladder (F6), mode-3 tee-ups (F9), and discount escalation all route through this one undocumented interaction. A base-architecture decision for a health-adjacent, exactly-once-mandated system should not rest on an interaction that neither vendor has documented. C1 needs no such interaction: its ask-first flow is an owned approval table.

### A2 — SERIOUS · DBOS version-pinning makes C2's long-sleeping workflows deploy-fragile (verified)
Detailed in §2.4. DBOS: *app version = code checksum; workflows tagged with a different version are not recovered; recommended remedy = blue-green with old-version workers draining to completion* (https://docs.dbos.dev/production/self-hosting/workflow-recovery). C2's L9 cadence = one durable-sleeping workflow per enrolled lead (hundreds live at any time, sleeping days). Consequence: **every deploy either orphans the live cadence book, forces multi-day dual-version operation on one droplet, or forces `DBOS__APPVERSION` pinning (accepting code-compat hazards the mechanism exists to prevent).** The honest fix is to move cadence state out of workflow checkpoints into tables driven by scheduled ticks — which is C1's design. C2's answer to its hardest operational problem converges to C1.

### A3 — SERIOUS · Pydantic AI churn is worse than C2 priced: 3-month breaking-change window, open design gaps in the exact surface C2 uses
Verified 2026-07-10: Pydantic AI hit v1 in September 2025 and is already on v2 (~9 months); the official version policy states the next major *"won't release sooner than 3 months after V2.0"* — the no-breaking-changes window was deliberately **shortened from six months to three** — and beta features are exempt from stability entirely: *"a feature's API and behaviors may not be stable, and it's very possible that changes... will not be backward-compatible"* (https://pydantic.dev/docs/ai/project/version-policy/; https://pydantic.dev/articles/pydantic-ai-v2). The deferred-tools surface C2 leans on is still under active design: issue #4302 (open since 2026-02-11, maintainers assigned, unresolved) shows deferred approval calls do not yet compose across nested agents/code-mode — the API is still growing load-bearing limbs (https://github.com/pydantic/pydantic-ai/issues/4302; see also #3959). A v3 with breaking changes could legally land mid-way through C2's own 13-week rollout. C1's exposure to this churn is zero-to-optional (§4, graft-back G-c).

### A4 — SERIOUS · Single-vendor, seed-stage substrate under 100% of the execution model
Verified 2026-07-10: DBOS Inc. — founded 2023, ~$8.5–9M raised, single seed round March 2024, no follow-on found (https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/dbos-inc; https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/571786-21). MIT license means no data hostage, but "worst case is vendoring" (C2 §2 caveat 4) means: a 1–3 person team with no platform engineer inherits maintenance of a durable-execution engine — checkpointing, recovery, queue semantics, the works. Under C1 the same vendor event costs a bounded subsystem with a pre-priced 2-week fallback; under C2 it costs the substrate of every function package ever written. R5 flagged exactly this: *"company viability is a real open question (small vendor)"* (research-R5.md §1). The risk is identical in kind for both candidates but wildly different in blast radius.

### A5 — SERIOUS · The precision-lens margin rests on uniformity rhetoric; the actual guards are the same code in both candidates
Read C2 §6 against C1 §5 side by side: send-intent outbox rows before transport (identical), `crm_write_log` WAL with hashed idempotency keys (identical — `hash(workflow_id|action|params)` vs `hash(turn_id|action|params)` is a key-naming difference, not a mechanism), Zoho upsert belt-and-suspenders (identical), twice-computed amounts + dollar regex guard (identical), content-hash window for WAHA (identical, and both call it "mitigated, not solved"), residual crash-mid-step-after-external-call window (identical, C2 §6.2 admits it). What C2 actually adds is checkpoint replay *between* the guards and mechanical key derivation. That is worth something (conceded, §5) — but the compliance judge's tiebreak treated C2's guards as structural and C1's as "discipline," when five of the seven listed guards are literally the same hand-written code C2 must also write and test. The 8.33-vs-7.67 gap that decided the tournament shrinks on inspection.

### A6 — SERIOUS · The spike gate de-risks the wrong things, and the fallback gets expensive after commitment
The Stage-0 spike tests four *integration mechanics* in ≤2 weeks: wrap, crash-recover, pause/resume, chaos-kill. It structurally cannot test the risks that actually distinguish C2: framework churn (A3) materializes at the next major, months in; vendor viability (A4) materializes on a funding cliff; deploy-versioning pain (A2) materializes on the *20th* deploy with a live cadence book, not in a clean spike sandbox. The synthesis converts "standing bet" into "two-week falsifiable test" — but only the mechanics are falsifiable in two weeks. Meanwhile G6's "C1 fallback without re-litigation" is priced as if it stays cheap; after Stage 2, packages exist as Pydantic-AI-typed objects and workflows, and the walk-back is no longer two weeks. The option value is asymmetric in C1's favor: **C1 can adopt C2's substrate incrementally later (wrap more of the turn in workflows if the guards prove error-prone); C2 cannot un-adopt cheaply.**

### A7 — MINOR · Port regression of the only validated behaviors
C2's own High risk #2. Eval-harness parity (generation + intent modes) plus turn_log replay covers response quality and routing; it does not cover conversation-state timing (mute-check races, group-command handling, dedup across restart during a live handoff). C2's mitigations are competent (strangler, 48h WAHA rollback) — hence MINOR — but C1 carries a zero on this line and the precision lens should have priced that asymmetry.

### A8 — MINOR · Hot-path checkpoint writes and pickle discipline are new failure surfaces on every turn
Every reactive turn in C2 runs as a workflow: a handful of checkpoint inserts per turn (C2 §3 prices 1–5 ms each — fine) plus pickle serialization of step outputs with a documented "must be picklable / large objects impact performance" constraint and a retry-configuration footgun (provider-client retries must be disabled, `max_retries=0`) per the integration docs (https://pydantic.dev/docs/ai/integrations/durable_execution/dbos/). None of this is disqualifying; all of it is debugging surface the single developer confronts at 3am *through framework internals* rather than through a greppable owned loop — the exact axis on which the pragmatist judge ranked C1 first.

## 4. Grafts C1 offers (importing C2's genuinely best ideas without the platform bet)

If C1 takes rank 1, it should absorb the following from C2 and the synthesis — all are compatible with C1's shape and none require Pydantic AI as the agent core:

- **G-a · Payload-by-reference / no-raw-text rule (C2 caveat 3 + synthesis G4)** for C1's own DBOS-run sagas: workflow inputs/step outputs carry only typed references (turn_log ids, enrollment ids, Zoho ids), never message text; CI check over workflow signatures + quarterly checkpoint-blob audit. C1's saga checkpoints have the same GDPR Art. 17 gap C2 spotted; close it identically.
- **G-b · One HITL primitive, owned:** a single `approval_requests` table + resume convention consumed by WriteGate ask-first, F9 tee-ups, and discount escalation — importing C2's "one approval mechanism serves three features" insight as ~200 lines of owned code and one Chatwoot card renderer (G1), instead of three bespoke flows.
- **G-c · Pydantic AI à la carte, inside TaskModules:** use Pydantic AI as a *library* for typed tools, structured-output slot extraction, and `pydantic-evals` Datasets inside C1's task modules — without DBOSAgent, without the reactor rewrite. C1 gets C2's typed-precision benefits where they pay (F9 slots, WriteGate payloads, graduation gates) while the orchestration spine stays owned. If churn bites (A3), the blast radius is a module, not the core.
- **G-d · Workflow-derived idempotency keys** for everything that *does* run under DBOS in C1 (sagas, cadence sends): adopt C2's mechanical `hash(workflow_id|...)` derivation there, keeping `turn_id`-derived keys only on the reactive path.
- **G-e · Import-time package validation:** define C1's function-package manifest as Pydantic models validated at registrar load — C2's "malformed packages fail at import, not in production" property, framework-free.
- **G-f · Selective turn durability:** any reactive turn that crosses into side-effect territory (write action, saga start, payment link) escalates its tail into a DBOS workflow; pure-conversational turns stay on the fast owned path. This neutralizes C2's "reactive turns run outside durable execution" attack at the only places it has teeth.

## 5. Honest concession — where C2 genuinely beats C1

Three things, stated without hedging:

1. **The single HITL primitive is a better idea than anything in C1 as written.** `requires_approval` → `DeferredToolRequests` → resume-the-same-workflow is architecturally cleaner than C1's implied trio of approval flows, and C2 found it first. G-b imports the insight, but an owned implementation will be a poorer cousin of a maintained framework feature — that is a real, recurring cost C1 accepts.
2. **Typed packages failing at import is a real extensibility edge.** The mission sentence is "continuously extensible company brain," and C2's framework-typed package contract is structurally stronger than C1's registrar discipline. G-e narrows but does not close this gap.
3. **If the spike passes cleanly and holds for a year, C2's compounding argument is true.** Uniform durability means every future function package inherits exactly-once discipline instead of re-implementing it, and C1's per-seam guards are a standing human-error surface — C1's own High risk #2 says so. C1's rebuttal is not that the premium is fake; it is that the premium is bought with verified standing exposures (A2–A4) that a 1–3 person team is the worst-positioned team in the industry to carry, and that C1 can buy most of the premium later, incrementally (G-f), once the pairing has a public track record instead of an undocumented hinge.

If NutriWhite hires a second backend engineer, or if the Stage-0 spike is extended to a 6-week pilot that also exercises a real deploy cycle over a live cadence book (directly testing A2), the balance shifts toward C2 and this advocate would soften.

## 6. The ask

Reverse the provisional verdict: **C1 as base architecture**, with grafts G1–G3, G7–G10 from the synthesis plus G-a…G-f above; retain C2's Stage-0 DBOS spike (C1 needs it too, scoped to sagas/cadence); document `TemporalAgent`/Temporal Cloud and full-C2 as the *scale-up* path rather than the starting bet. The 0.07 margin was purchased with an undocumented integration, an unpriced deploy tax, a 3-month breaking-change window, and a seed-stage substrate under 100% of execution — against the study's own R5 recommendation and the largest pragmatism gap on the board. For this business, at this team size, under this precision mandate: evolve, don't re-platform.
